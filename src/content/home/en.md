Before going independent, I wrote code for many years, working my way up from junior developer to architect. You'd think building products would be exactly my strength, and it was—until my own product shipped and I saw how little that proved. Building something well and having someone need it are two different things. It took me about a year to admit that.

So when something looks obvious now, I've gotten into the habit of asking: does it really have to work this way? Not to pick fights. It's just that when an answer is that common, I want to know why it held in the first place, and whether it still does. AI deserves extra caution here—it hands you a well-reasoned analysis within minutes, and it feels like understanding. Most of the time it's only a nicer explanation.

There's no shortcut to understanding. You put the judgment out there and see what actually happens. This site holds the [products](/projects) I make, and the [notes](/notes) I write along the way. Some turned out right, some wrong. They all stay.

Answers change. The questions stay. That's what this website keeps a record of.
